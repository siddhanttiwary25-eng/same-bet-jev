Why this is hard

Near-identical titles, different payouts

These pairs read almost the same, but they settle differently. A title matcher sees one market. A trader who treats them as one holds an unhedged position.

    %%traps%%
Fine print

Jev caught %%n_fixed%% pairs I labeled wrong

On its first run Jev refused to match some markets with word-for-word identical titles. I had labeled them the same bet. Reading the full rules showed Jev was right: the two venues pay out on different triggers. I corrected those labels, applied the same correction to every pair with the same rule pattern, and wrote down each reason.

Method

How this was built

  • Pulled every open Polymarket market (top 2,100 by volume) and Kalshi market (about 200,000) from their public APIs.
  • For each Polymarket market, kept the two Kalshi markets with the most similar titles. That gives 2,434 hard look-alike pairs. A random 200 were sampled across similarity bands.
  • Labeled each pair same, variant or different from the full resolution rules. Labels were made with AI help and reviewed by me; 3 unclear pairs were dropped.
  • Jev got both markets' question and rules, and four questions: one choice (same / variant / different) and three yes/no checks. Its top choice is used as-is, with no threshold tuned.
  • The first-draft instructions were too strict: zero false matches, but they missed most real twins. I rewrote them using a separate 40-pair practice set that shares no markets with the test set (missed matches there fell from 11 to 2), locked them, and ran the test set once.
  • After the first run I reread the full rules of every pair Jev confidently disagreed with and corrected 10 labels where the payout rules differ. Scores against the original labels are shown too. I reviewed Jev's disagreements more closely than its agreements, which could flatter it slightly.
  • Not tested yet: a general-purpose LLM baseline, and a larger labeled set. Both are the first step of a real trial.

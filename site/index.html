Why this is hard

Near-identical titles, different payouts

These pairs read almost the same, but they settle differently. A title matcher sees one market. A trader who treats them as one holds an unhedged position.

    Fine print

    Jev caught 10 pairs I labeled wrong

    On its first run Jev refused to match some markets with word-for-word identical titles. I had labeled them the same bet. Reading the full rules showed Jev was right: the two venues pay out on different triggers. I corrected those labels, applied the same correction to every pair with the same rule pattern, and wrote down each reason.

    • Vanta IPO before 2027?
      identical-looking on both venues · title similarity 100/100

      Kalshi resolves YES when the S-1 goes effective, the IPO is priced, or a ticker is assigned; Polymarket needs a completed first sale of stock. Different payout trigger.

    • Will Naftali Bennett be the next Prime Minister of Israel?
      identical-looking on both venues · title similarity 100/100

      Kalshi counts acting/interim PMs and has no 2027 cutoff; Polymarket excludes interim PMs and resolves 'Other' after Dec 31, 2027. Different payout conditions.

    • Israel and Lebanon normalize relations before 2027?
      identical-looking on both venues · title similarity 100/100

      Kalshi requires a ratified agreement; Polymarket only requires both governments to announce diplomatic relations. Different payout trigger.

    A title matcher scores these pairs 100/100. A trader who hedged one venue with the other would be exposed exactly when it matters.

    Objections

    "We already use GPT or Claude for this."

    Jev returns a structured choice plus probabilities, with no prompt parsing, at $0.042 per million input tokens and free output. The trial runs both side by side on your pairs, so the comparison uses your data, not ours.

    "A wrong match costs us money."

    Route by confidence. Act automatically only when Jev is confident; send the rest to a reviewer. The three yes/no checks (same subject, same terms, same deadline) tell the reviewer exactly why a pair failed.

    "Our markets are different from your demo."

    That is why the trial starts with 200 to 500 of your own pairs and a locked test set we agree on before running anything.

    Two-week proof of concept

    The same steps I used here, packaged so any prospect can run them on their own data. The repo is the starter kit.

    2. Days 1-2

      Pull 200 to 500 candidate pairs from their catalog. Label them together, or use pairs they already mapped. Split into a dev set and a locked test set.

      Map their market fields into Jev's state. Start from this repo's question set. Tune instructions on the dev set only.

      Run Jev next to their current method on live traffic. Log every disagreement and review it with their team.

    5. Days 11-14

      Readout

      Score the locked test set once. Report accuracy, false matches, missed matches, reviewer hours saved, and cost at their volume. Ask for the decision.

    Method

    How this was built

    • Pulled every open Polymarket market (top 2,100 by volume) and Kalshi market (about 200,000) from their public APIs.
    • For each Polymarket market, kept the two Kalshi markets with the most similar titles. That gives 2,434 hard look-alike pairs. A random 200 were sampled across similarity bands.
    • Labeled each pair same, variant or different from the full resolution rules. Labels were made with AI help and reviewed by me; 3 unclear pairs were dropped.
    • Jev got both markets' question and rules, and four questions: one choice (same / variant / different) and three yes/no checks. Its top choice is used as-is, with no threshold tuned.
    • The first-draft instructions were too strict: zero false matches, but they missed most real twins. I rewrote them using a separate 40-pair practice set that shares no markets with the test set (missed matches there fell from 11 to 2), locked them, and ran the test set once.
    • After the first run I reread the full rules of every pair Jev confidently disagreed with and corrected 10 labels where the payout rules differ. Scores against the original labels are shown too. I reviewed Jev's disagreements more closely than its agreements, which could flatter it slightly.
    • Not tested yet: a general-purpose LLM baseline, and a larger labeled set. Both are the first step of a real trial.
